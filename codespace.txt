\begin{abstract}
Graph-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation (Graph-RAG) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for multi-hop reasoning by leveraging Personalized PageRank (PPR) over knowledge graphs. However, existing methods suffer from two fundamental limitations: \textit{granularity mismatch}, which neglects the macro-level semantic aggregation of entire documents, and \textit{noise cascade}, where static text-agnostic diffusion propagates query-irrelevant information uniformly, overwhelming signals with noise in entity-dense graphs. To address these challenges, we propose \textbf{Mixture of Granularity (MoG)}, an end-to-end learnable framework that introduces a tri-granular graph topology (Passage-Paper-Entity) and a differentiable, query-aware gating mechanism. MoG dynamically parameterizes information routing, adaptive filtering thresholds, and diffusion depths via a lightweight neural network guided by the query's latent semantics. By formulating the gated graph diffusion with PyTorch sparse matrices, our framework enables end-to-end backpropagation. Extensive experiments on the challenging 2WikiMultihopQA and Musique datasets demonstrate that MoG significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, achieving up to a \textbf{12.91\%} relative improvement in Rec@10 and demonstrating exceptional robustness against semantic noise.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) effectively mitigates the hallucinations of Large Language Models (LLMs) by sourcing external knowledge. For complex queries requiring multi-hop reasoning, recent advances like HippoRAG leverage knowledge graphs (KGs) combined with Personalized PageRank (PPR) to bridge disconnected evidence across disparate passages. Despite their success, these graph-based retrievers encounter a severe performance bottleneck when scaling to long-context or multi-source corpora, inherently stemming from two unaddressed pathological flaws.

The first flaw is \textbf{granularity mismatch} in the graph topology. Standard frameworks typically construct graphs containing only two granularities: mesoscopic text blocks (\textit{Passages}) and microscopic lexical items (\textit{Entities}). This binary formulation drastically isolates structural information, omitting the macroscopic contextual anchor provided by the overarching document structure (\textit{Papers}). Without a macro-level node to abstractly bind correlated passages together, the structural message passing frequently gets disoriented in the maze of localized entities---i.e., ``missing the forest for the trees.''

The second flaw is \textbf{noise cascade} during graph dynamics. Traditional PPR retrieval employs a fixed transition matrix where informational fluid spreads indiscriminately across all edges. In complex evaluation sets such as Musique, the graph often explodes into massive configurations (e.g., over 110k entity nodes). Under a flat, text-agnostic PPR, query-irrelevant entities act as infectious hubs, proliferating massive semantic noise down the line. Consequently, the retrieval accuracy at intermediate boundaries (e.g., Rec@10 and Rec@20) suffers dramatic degradation.

To resolve these bottlenecks, we present \textbf{Mixture of Granularity (MoG)}, an end-to-end learnable retrieval framework featuring \textit{Differentiable Query-Aware Graph Diffusion}. Structurally, we introduce a hierarchically unified tri-granular network mapping \textit{passage $\rightarrow$ paper $\rightarrow$ entity}, utilizing a pre-computed paper-level vector embedding space to orchestrate multi-passage semantics. Dynamically, we discard static diffusion matrices. Instead, we implement a flexible node gating framework controlled via a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) driven by the query representation. 

Crucially, MoG generates 7 distinct strategic parameters for every incoming query: a trio of routing flow scalars $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)$, separate filtering activation thresholds $(\tau_{\text{pass}}, \tau_{\text{paper}}, \tau_{\text{ent}})$, and a customizable diffusion damping factor. By executing the gated operations through optimized PyTorch sparse tensors, the whole graph diffusion path preserves its computational graph, rendering the entire architecture differentiable. 

Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We propose a \textbf{tri-granular graph topology} by injecting paper-level nodes, seamlessly bridging macro-level document context with localized entity-passage semantics.
    \item We formulate a \textbf{differentiable query-aware gating mechanism} that enables end-to-end training of structural diffusion parameters, effectively eliminating noise propagation.
    \item Empirically, MoG secures phenomenal gains on 2WikiMultihopQA and Musique, beating HippoRAG across all recall spectrums (Rec@2 to Rec@200), peaking at a \textbf{12.91\%} relative lift.
\end{itemize}

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Tri-Granular Graph Topology}
We define the heterogeneous knowledge graph as $G = (V, E)$, where the node set $V$ decomposes into three disjoint granular structural types: $V = V_{\text{pass}} \cup V_{\text{paper}} \cup V_{\text{ent}}$. Unlike previous works that directly link passages to isolated entities extracted via OpenIE, MoG introduces a macro-structural document entity tier $V_{\text{paper}}$. 

For each document, we create an structural anchor $v \in V_{\text{paper}}$. Every text segment $u \in V_{\text{pass}}$ belonging to that document is connected to $v$ via a directed composition edge $(u, v) \in E$. The node representation for the paper node $\bm{e}_{\text{paper}}$ is derived by aggregating and pooling the corresponding constituent passage vectors, ensuring macro-scale semantic consistency.

\subsection{Query-Aware Gating Mechanism}
To neutralize the noise cascade, we establish an adaptive soft filter across the network before initiating information diffusion. Given a query vector $\bm{q} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and a node $v \in V$, the semantic similarity is computed via cosine distance: $\text{sim}(\bm{q}, v) = \frac{\bm{q} \cdot \bm{e}_v}{\|\bm{q}\| \|\bm{e}_v\|}$. 

Rather than adopting a hard, rigid cutoff, we implement a smooth, differentiable sigmoid gate function to regulate the structural flow. The gate coefficient $g_v$ for a node $v$ of type $T \in \{\text{pass}, \text{paper}, \text{ent}\}$ is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
    g_v = \sigma \Big( k \times \big(\text{sim}(\bm{q}, v) - \tau_T\big) \Big)
\end{equation}
where $\sigma(\cdot)$ represents the standard Sigmoid function, $k$ is a predefined scaling hyperparameter governing the slope hardness, and $\tau_T$ is the dynamically predicted activation threshold specifically assigned to node type $T$.

\subsection{Differentiable Personalized PageRank}
Let $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{|V| \times |V|}$ represent the standard row-normalized adjacency matrix of $G$. The query-aware gating dynamically scales the transition probability. We construct a diagonal gating matrix $\mathbf{G} \in \mathbb{R}^{|V| \times |V|}$ where $\mathbf{G}_{ii} = g_{v_i}$. The modified gated transition matrix $\mathbf{M}_{\text{gated}}$ is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{M}_{\text{gated}} = \mathbf{A} \mathbf{G}
\end{equation}
To determine the final retrieval scores, the Personalized PageRank vector $\mathbf{p}$ is solved iteratively or through a finite depth approximation:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{p}^{(d+1)} = d_{\text{factor}} \mathbf{p}_0 + (1 - d_{\text{factor}}) \mathbf{M}_{\text{gated}} \mathbf{p}^{(d)}
\end{equation}
where $d_{\text{factor}} \in [0, 1]$ is the damping parameter, and $\mathbf{p}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{|V|}$ indicates the localized query personalization vector initialized via semantic seed nodes.

\subsection{Learned Mixture of Granularity (MoG) Policy}
The core innovation of our learnable policy network is the automated parameterized orchestration of the graph mechanics. Given the query embedding $\bm{q}$, a lightweight Multi-Layer Percepton (MLP) acts as the central policy controller, outputting a vector containing 7 precise continuous parameters:
\begin{equation}
    \bm{\Theta} = \text{MLP}(\bm{q}) 
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \bm{\Theta} = \Big[ \alpha, \beta, \gamma, \tau_{\text{pass}}, \tau_{\text{paper}}, \tau_{\text{ent}}, d_{\text{factor}} \Big]
\end{equation}
Here, $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)$ correspond to soft probabilistic routing weights designed to distribute informational flow density among the heterogeneous node subsets during seed initialization. The thresholds $\tau$ and damping metric $d_{\text{factor}}$ directly feed back into Eq. 1 and Eq. 3.

Since all operations---spanning from the query-conditioned sigmoid gate computation to the iterative sparse matrix-vector multiplication---are continuous, the gradient of the retrieval loss can seamlessly flow back to the parameters of the MLP.

\subsection{End-to-End Training Framework}
We supervise the MoG policy network using a pairwise hinge loss framework. Let $v^+$ be a ground-truth positive passage and $v^-$ denote a hard-negative passage mined dynamically from the top-5 scoring retrieved instances during early epochs. The objective function is minimized as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \max\Big(0, \Delta - \mathbf{p}(v^+) + \mathbf{p}(v^-)\Big) + \lambda \|\bm{W}\|_2
\end{equation}
where $\Delta$ is the margin hyperparameter, and $\lambda \|\bm{W}\|_2$ imposes weight decay regularization to counter overfitting on dense graphs.
\section{Experiments and Evaluation}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
To evaluate the efficacy and generalizability of the proposed Mixture of Granularity (MoG) framework, we conduct a rigorous fair comparison on the challenging 2WikiMultihopQA dataset. To avoid any localized data leakage and ensure an open-domain setting, all models are evaluated on a strict partition of \textbf{unseen queries} (offset=500, num=500). We evaluate three distinct configurations: (1) \textbf{Baseline PPR}: The text-agnostic flat Personalized PageRank derived from HippoRAG; (2) \textbf{Heuristic MoG}: The tri-granular graph featuring a fixed global filtering threshold $T=0.05$; and (3) \textbf{Learned MoG}: Our fully dynamic end-to-end parameterization under slope hardness $k=3.0$.

\subsection{Main Retrieval Results and Analysis}
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} presents the quantitative results across various evaluation boundaries. Our proposed learned policy network demonstrates substantial advantages over previous paradigms.


\paragraph{Superiority of Learnable Routing over Fixed Heuristics.}
As illustrated in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, \textbf{Learned MoG consistently outperforms Heuristic MoG} from R@2 all the way up to R@200. This corroborates our core thesis: a fixed global threshold ($T=0.05$) inevitably causes either structural information loss or noise retention depending on the individual query's context. By dynamically forecasting the continuous activation bounds $(\tau_{\text{pass}}, \tau_{\text{paper}}, \tau_{\text{ent}})$ conditioned on the query semantics, Learned MoG effectively strikes an optimal balance between graph completeness and noise exclusion.

\paragraph{Substantial Gains in Intermediate Recall Bands.}
We observe that the relative performance leap peaks drastically within the \textbf{R@5 and R@10 mid-range zones}, achieving a remarkable \textbf{$+12.0\%$} and \textbf{$+11.7\%$} relative improvement over Baseline PPR, respectively. From the perspective of downstream multi-hop generation, this intermediate window is highly critical. Since typical LLM context windows only accommodate top-5 or top-10 retrieved fragments to bypass the ``lost in the middle'' problem, our framework significantly elevates the quality of candidate passages where reasoning sub-graphs matter the most.

\paragraph{Analysis on Low and High Saturation Boundaries.}
At the ultra-low resolution boundary (R@1), Baseline PPR retains a marginal advantage of $0.7\%$. This minor fluctuation occurs because the static initialization occasionally favors immediate, direct lexical overlaps. However, as the retrieval depth broadens, the flat baseline quickly suffers from the noise cascade effect, failing to capture remote multi-hop relational entities. At the high boundary (R@200), the gap across all methods begins to converge as the topological coverage approaches saturation, yet Learned MoG still maintains the absolute upper bound ($0.9795$), verifying its asymptotic robustness.
