# QG-PPR: Query-Conditioned Gated PPR for Multi-Granular Graph Retrieval ## 1. 요지 **QG-PPR (Query-Conditioned Gated Personalized PageRank)** discloses a query-conditioned graph retrieval method that dynamically controls both **relevance injection** and **graph propagation** in Personalized PageRank (PPR) over a multi-granular knowledge graph. Instead of applying a fixed PPR diffusion strategy to all queries, QG-PPR uses a lightweight learned policy network conditioned on the query embedding to generate query-specific retrieval parameters. The policy controls granularity-aware routing weights, node-level selectivity parameters, and diffusion persistence, thereby allowing different queries to follow different graph retrieval strategies. The core technical contribution is the decomposition of query-conditioned PPR control into two complementary mechanisms: 1. **Query-Conditioned Relevance Injection:** query-dependent gates and granularity routing weights regulate how much relevance mass is injected into entity, passage, and paper nodes at initialization. 2. **Query-Conditioned Propagation:** the same query-conditioned gating mechanism regulates the propagation of relevance through the graph, continuously suppressing irrelevant nodes and diffusion paths. These two mechanisms can be used independently or jointly, resulting in the following retrieval variants: * **M0 — Vanilla PPR:** fixed PPR without query-conditioned gating. * **M1 — Query-Conditioned Injection:** adaptive personalization/relevance injection without adaptive propagation. * **M2 — Query-Conditioned Propagation:** adaptive propagation gating without adaptive personalization. * **M3 — QG-PPR:** joint query-conditioned relevance injection and propagation gating. The complete QG-PPR model corresponds to M3 and jointly learns **where relevance should enter the graph and how relevance should propagate through the graph**. --- # 2. 확보 가능한 권리 범위 ### Claim 1 — Query-conditioned multi-granular graph retrieval A graph-based retrieval method comprising: 1. constructing or receiving a multi-granular knowledge graph containing at least entity, passage, and document-level nodes; 2. receiving a query and generating a query embedding; 3. computing query-conditioned relevance signals between the query embedding and graph nodes; 4. generating a query-conditioned personalization vector for PPR; 5. performing personalized graph diffusion over the knowledge graph; and 6. ranking passage nodes according to the resulting PPR scores. ### Claim 2 — Query-conditioned learned policy The method of Claim 1, wherein a learned policy network receives the query embedding and predicts a plurality of retrieval-control parameters, including one or more of: * granularity-specific routing weights; * node-type-specific gate thresholds; * node-type-specific gate slopes; * diffusion damping or persistence parameters; and * other query-conditioned graph diffusion parameters. Although Personalized PageRank provides a mechanism for query-dependent personalization, its propagation behavior is generally governed by fixed graph transition rules and manually selected diffusion parameters. QG-PPR addresses this limitation by introducing **query-conditioned control over both relevance injection and relevance propagation**. The key idea is that different queries may require different graph retrieval behaviors. For example, one query may primarily require passage-level evidence, while another may require document-level or entity-level intermediate reasoning. Likewise, some queries may benefit from broader graph diffusion, whereas others require aggressive suppression of irrelevant high-degree nodes. QG-PPR therefore learns a retrieval policy conditioned on the query rather than applying a single fixed diffusion configuration to all queries. --- # 4. 선행 기술과의 차이점 | Aspect | Conventional / Vanilla PPR | QG-PPR | | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | | Query conditioning | Mainly used for initial personalization | Used to control both injection and propagation | | Personalization | Fixed retrieval construction | Query-conditioned relevance injection | | Node filtering | No query-conditioned filtering | Node- and granularity-specific sigmoid gating | | Propagation | Uniform graph diffusion | Query-conditioned gated diffusion | | Granularity routing | Fixed or uniform | Query-conditioned routing weights | | Gate parameters | Fixed/manual or absent | Query-conditioned thresholds and slopes | | Damping | Fixed or globally selected | Query-conditioned diffusion persistence | | Noise suppression | No explicit propagation control | Continuous suppression of irrelevant diffusion | | Training | Retrieval and diffusion often separately designed | Differentiable policy optimization through PPR | | Retrieval policy | Same diffusion strategy for different queries | Query-specific graph retrieval strategy | The fundamental distinction is therefore not merely the introduction of a learned PPR parameter, but the introduction of a **query-conditioned control mechanism over both relevance injection and graph propagation**. --- # 5. Patent Technology Domain QG-PPR belongs to the field of: > **Graph-based information retrieval using query-conditioned Personalized PageRank and multi-granular knowledge graphs.** The technology is particularly applicable to GraphRAG and multi-hop retrieval systems in which relevant evidence may be distributed across heterogeneous graph nodes. The invention introduces a **Query-Conditioned Gated PPR framework** in which a learned policy transforms a query embedding into a retrieval-specific diffusion strategy. The policy can dynamically determine: * which graph granularities should receive greater relevance; * which nodes should contribute strongly to graph diffusion; * which nodes should be suppressed; * how aggressively relevance should propagate; and * how much diffusion persistence should be used for the current query. This converts PPR from a largely fixed graph diffusion mechanism into a **query-conditioned retrieval policy**. --- # 6. Motivation — Why QG-PPR? ## Problem 1: Fixed graph diffusion Graph-based retrieval systems such as PPR-based GraphRAG methods can propagate relevance through multiple hops of a knowledge graph. However, a fixed propagation strategy may indiscriminately transmit relevance through both useful and irrelevant nodes. This is particularly problematic in dense knowledge graphs containing: * high-degree entities; * semantically related but answer-irrelevant passages; * documents sharing common terminology; * noisy cross-document connections; and * heterogeneous relations across multiple semantic granularities. As diffusion proceeds, irrelevant relevance can cascade through the graph and eventually dominate the top-ranked passages. --- ## Problem 2: Query-independent propagation behavior Different queries require different retrieval behaviors. For example: * a highly specific process query may require narrow diffusion around a small number of relevant entities; * a document-level literature query may benefit from broader propagation through paper-level nodes; * a cross-document technical query may require entity-to-paper-to-passage diffusion. A single fixed PPR configuration cannot optimally represent these different retrieval requirements. --- ## Problem 3: Multi-granular graph noise A multi-granular graph contains different types of nodes with substantially different semantic roles. For example: [ \text{Entity} \rightarrow \text{Passage} \rightarrow \text{Paper} \rightarrow \text{Passage}. ] The importance of each granularity depends on the query. A query about a specific process chemistry may require strong entity-level signals, while a query concerning a broader technology trend may benefit from paper-level evidence. QG-PPR therefore learns query-conditioned granularity routing instead of assuming equal importance among node types. --- # 7. Summary of the Invention The present invention discloses a **Query-Conditioned Gated Personalized PageRank (QG-PPR)** method and system for multi-granular graph retrieval. The core idea is: > **Replace fixed graph diffusion with a query-conditioned retrieval policy that controls both relevance injection and relevance propagation.** The invention consists of four principal components. ### 7.1 Multi-Granular Knowledge Graph The graph contains heterogeneous semantic granularities, including: * entity nodes; * passage nodes; and * paper/document nodes. Paper-level nodes provide document-level structural anchors that connect semantically related passages within the same document. This enables retrieval to reason across multiple semantic scales rather than relying exclusively on passage-level or entity-level connections. --- ### 7.2 Query-Conditioned Granularity Routing A lightweight policy network receives the query embedding: [ q_e = \operatorname{Embed}(q) ] and predicts granularity-specific routing weights: [ \mathbf{w}(q) ============= [ \alpha(q), \beta(q), \gamma(q) ]. ] The weights are normalized using softmax: [ \mathbf{w}(q) ============= \operatorname{softmax} ( \Theta_{1:3} ). ] These weights determine how strongly entity, passage, and paper nodes contribute to the query-conditioned retrieval process. Thus, different queries can dynamically emphasize different graph granularities. --- ### 7.3 Query-Conditioned Node Gating For every graph node (v), the system computes query-node similarity: [ s(q,v) ====== \cos(e_q,e_v). ] A node-type-specific sigmoid gate is then calculated: [ g_v(q) ====== \sigma \left( k_{T(v)}(q) [ s(q,v)-\tau_{T(v)}(q) ] \right). ] The gate provides a soft relevance filter. Unlike hard filtering, the mechanism does not remove graph nodes. Instead, it continuously scales their contribution according to their relevance to the current query. This allows the model to suppress irrelevant nodes while retaining potentially useful weak signals. --- # 8. Query-Conditioned Relevance Injection The first role of the gate is to control **where relevance enters the graph**. Given an initial personalization vector (r), QG-PPR constructs: [ r_q(v) ====== r(v) \cdot w_{T(v)}(q) \cdot g_v(q). ] Consequently, the initial relevance distribution is different for different queries. A query emphasizing entity-level reasoning can produce a large entity contribution, whereas another query can assign greater relevance to paper or passage nodes. This mechanism corresponds to **M1 — Query-Conditioned Injection**. The key property is: > **M1 learns where to start graph diffusion.** --- # 9. Query-Conditioned Propagation The second role of the gate is to control **where relevance propagates**. During graph diffusion, the query-conditioned gate can modulate the current relevance distribution before propagation: [ p^{(t+1)} ========= d_q r_q + (1-d_q) M ( p^{(t)} \odot g_q ). ] Therefore, a node with low query relevance contributes less relevance to subsequent propagation. This prevents irrelevant nodes from repeatedly transmitting noisy relevance through multiple graph hops. This mechanism corresponds to **M2 — Query-Conditioned Propagation**. The key property is: > **M2 learns where to propagate.** --- # 10. Joint QG-PPR The complete QG-PPR model combines both mechanisms: [ \boxed{ \text{Query-Conditioned Injection} + \text{Query-Conditioned Propagation} } ] The resulting model corresponds to **M3**. Conceptually: [ \boxed{ \text{Query} \rightarrow \text{Policy} \rightarrow \begin{cases} \text{Where to inject relevance}\ \text{Where to propagate relevance} \end{cases} \rightarrow \text{PPR} \rightarrow \text{Passage Ranking} } ] This allows the retrieval system to simultaneously control the initial relevance distribution and subsequent diffusion trajectory. --- # 11. Query-Conditioned Diffusion Persistence The policy additionally predicts a query-conditioned PPR damping parameter: [ d_q === d_{\min} + (d_{\max}-d_{\min}) \sigma(\theta_d). ] The parameter controls the balance between query-conditioned relevance injection and graph propagation. A higher propagation persistence allows relevance to travel further through the graph, while a stronger restart component keeps retrieval closer to the query-conditioned initial relevance distribution. Thus, QG-PPR can dynamically adjust the effective diffusion behavior for different queries. --- # 12. Differentiable QG-PPR Training To optimize the query-conditioned retrieval policy, PPR is implemented using differentiable sparse matrix operations. Let (M) denote the graph transition operator. The propagation process can be expressed as: [ p^{(t+1)} ========= d_q r_q + (1-d_q) M ( p^{(t)} \odot g_q ). ] Because the graph propagation and gating operations are differentiable, gradients can propagate from the final passage ranking objective back to: * routing weights; * gate thresholds; * gate slopes; * damping parameters; and * the policy network. This enables direct optimization of the query-conditioned retrieval policy. --- # 13. Pairwise Ranking Objective For each query, a ground-truth passage (v^+) and a hard negative passage (v^-) are constructed. The policy is optimized using a pairwise hinge loss: [ \mathcal{L} =========== \max \left( 0, \Delta ------ p_q(v^+) + p_q(v^-) \right). ] The objective directly encourages: [ p_q(v^+) > p_q(v^-)+\Delta. ] Hard negatives are selected from high-ranked non-ground-truth passages produced by the retrieval system. Such negatives are particularly useful because they are often semantically similar to the query but do not contain the required evidence. The policy is therefore trained to distinguish: > **semantically related nodes** from: > **retrieval-relevant nodes**. --- # 14. Training Data Construction For each training query: ### Positive node The positive node (v^+) corresponds to a ground-truth passage annotated as containing the required evidence. ### Hard negative node A hard negative (v^-) is selected from highly ranked non-ground-truth passages. The training example is therefore: [ (q,v^+,v^-). ] The pairwise objective directly optimizes the relative PPR ranking of these passages. This design is particularly suitable for graph retrieval because the most difficult retrieval errors are often caused by nodes that are structurally or semantically close to the correct evidence. --- # 15. Three Key Technical Effects ## 16.1 Query-conditioned relevance allocation QG-PPR dynamically allocates relevance among different graph granularities according to the query. Therefore, the same graph can be traversed differently for different queries. --- ## 16.2 Query-conditioned noise suppression The sigmoid gate continuously attenuates relevance associated with low-query-relevance nodes. This reduces the likelihood that irrelevant nodes initiate or sustain multi-hop noise propagation. The mechanism is particularly important for top-K retrieval, where even a small number of highly ranked irrelevant passages can significantly affect downstream generation. --- ## 16.3 Joint injection and propagation control The strongest configuration combines both mechanisms. The empirical results demonstrate that the joint model provides substantially better recall than either mechanism alone. For example, the current experimental results show: [ R@10: \quad M1=65.43%, \quad M2=69.80%, \quad M3=73.60%. ] Thus, the joint mechanism improves over M1 by: [ +8.17 ] percentage points, and over M2 by: [ +3.80 ] percentage points at Recall@10. At Recall@20: [ M1=72.87%, \quad M2=76.13%, \quad M3=79.73%. ] At Recall@50: [ M1=84.15%, \quad M2=84.93%, \quad M3=88.52%. ] These results support the complementary roles of relevance injection and propagation control. --- # 17. Difference from Conventional PPR-Based Retrieval Conventional PPR-based retrieval can be represented conceptually as: [ q \rightarrow r \rightarrow PPR(G,r) \rightarrow Ranking. ] The graph transition mechanism is largely independent of the query. QG-PPR instead performs: [ q \rightarrow \pi_\theta(q) \rightarrow \begin{cases} r_q\ g_q\ d_q \end{cases} \rightarrow PPR(G,r_q,g_q,d_q) \rightarrow Ranking. ] Therefore, QG-PPR changes the retrieval paradigm from: > **one fixed diffusion strategy for all queries** to: > **a query-specific graph diffusion strategy for each query.** --- # 18.
